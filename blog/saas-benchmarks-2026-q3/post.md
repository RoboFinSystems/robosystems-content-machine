Last quarter, 19 public B2B SaaS companies kept between 61 and 85 cents of each revenue dollar after the cost of delivering their product, and spent between 16 and 44 cents of it on sales and marketing. The middle half kept 71 to 81 cents and spent 22 to 40.

These are the numbers a private SaaS company gets compared against, by its board, its investors and its buyers. Here they are for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, read from each company's 10-Q, with every row opening to the filing it came from.

## The benchmark

| Measure | Median | Middle half | Range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue growth, year over year | 16.0% | 8.5% to 23.7% | -2.5% to 35.2% |
| Gross margin | 76.6% | 71.1% to 81.1% | 60.8% to 85.3% |
| Sales and marketing, share of revenue | 33.6% | 22.4% to 40.1% | 15.8% to 43.6% |
| Research and development, share of revenue | 22.5% | 16.1% to 25.3% | 8.4% to 42.8% |
| General and administrative, share of revenue | 13.1% | 10.1% to 15.6% | 7.5% to 19.9% |
| Operating margin (GAAP) | 7.6% | -0.7% to 16.5% | -25.2% to 35.3% |

"Middle half" runs from the 5th to the 15th of the 19 companies when they are ranked on that measure. All figures are GAAP as reported, so stock-based compensation sits inside each expense line.

## Growth by sales spend

Split the 19 by how much of revenue went to sales and marketing, and compare median growth:

| Sales and marketing, share of revenue | Companies | Median growth |
|---|---:|---:|
| Under 25% | 5 | 10.7% |
| 25% to 35% | 5 | 17.4% |
| 35% or more | 9 | 17.1% |

The five that spent under 25 cents grew slowest. Above that, the heavier spenders did not grow faster. The fastest grower, Datadog at 35%, spent 25 cents of each revenue dollar on sales and marketing, and 41 cents on research and development. The four companies that spent 42 cents or more grew between 17% and 25%. One quarter and 19 companies is a pattern worth asking about, not a law: growth reflects years of past spending, and product-led companies book some selling cost elsewhere.

## How these numbers were built

- **The filings.** Each company's 10-Q for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, from the RoboSystems SEC repository, a knowledge graph of 8,000+ SEC filers' XBRL filings. Every company's quarter is the same three months. For Atlassian, Intapp and Paylocity it is the first quarter of fiscal 2027, and for Dynatrace the second; for the rest it is calendar Q3.
- **The companies.** These 19 are the peer sets for six kinds of private software company (marketing and sales, finance and HR, security, developer and data tools, vertical software, analytics and workflow). They are not the whole sector: larger filers such as ServiceNow are outside these sets, and companies whose fiscal quarter ends in another month (Intuit, Okta and Veeva among them) are out so that every row covers the same three months.
- **The facts.** Consolidated totals only, the three months ended September 30, against the same three months of 2025. Revenue is each company's own revenue line; gross margin is gross profit over revenue (or revenue less cost of revenue where no gross profit line is tagged).
- **Where the companies differ.** Paylocity's revenue of $451.7 million includes $27.2 million that is not from contracts with customers (interest on funds held for clients), about 6% of the total; without it, gross margin is 65.2% rather than 67.3%. Blackbaud has the lowest gross margin of the 19; read its revenue note before putting a subscription business beside it. Varonis tags revenue including assessed taxes.
- **Left out.** Four companies whose revenue mix differs from subscription software: BILL (float and payments revenue), Alarm.com (hardware), Bandwidth (usage-based communications) and Manhattan Associates (a large services business).
